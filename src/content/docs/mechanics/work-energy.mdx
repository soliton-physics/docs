## Head-on collision

<Scenario id="collision-1d">
<div slot="intuition">
Momentum is always conserved in a collision. Energy is a different story: in a perfectly elastic collision the
kinetic energy is also conserved, in a perfectly inelastic one the two bodies stick together and as much energy as
possible is lost to heat and deformation. The coefficient of restitution $e$ dials between the two. Watch the
dissipated bar in the energy panel jump at the moment of impact.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
Momentum conservation and the definition of $e$ (separation speed over approach speed) give

$$
v_1 = \frac{m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 - m_2 e (u_1 - u_2)}{m_1 + m_2}, \qquad
  v_2 = \frac{m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 + m_1 e (u_1 - u_2)}{m_1 + m_2}.
$$

The kinetic energy lost is $\tfrac{1}{2}\frac{m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2}(1 - e^2)(u_1 - u_2)^2$.
</div>
<div slot="engine">
The impact is an impulse solved at the velocity level with Newton's restitution rule. The energy it removes is
computed exactly from the impulse and credited to the dissipation ledger, so the energy panel balances to 1e-10.
</div>
</Scenario>

## Bob in a vertical circle

<Scenario id="vertical-circle">
<div slot="intuition">
Swing a bob on a string in a vertical circle. At the top the string can only pull, never push, so if the bob is too
slow the string goes slack and the bob falls inward instead of completing the circle. There is a minimum speed at the
bottom that just makes it over the top. Set the speed slider a little below it and watch the event log.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
At the top, $T + m g = m v_{top}^2 / L$. The string stays taut while $v_{top}^2 \ge g L$. Energy conservation between
bottom and top, $v_{bottom}^2 = v_{top}^2 + 4 g L$, gives the minimum

$$
v_{bottom} = \sqrt{5 g L}.
$$

Tension at the bottom is $m (v^2/L + g)$.
</div>
<div slot="engine">
The string is a rope constraint, so it cannot push. When the required tension would be negative the engine lets the
rope go slack and emits a `rope_slack` event. Validated: the event appears when launched at 95 % of the critical speed
and not at 105 %.
</div>
</Scenario>
