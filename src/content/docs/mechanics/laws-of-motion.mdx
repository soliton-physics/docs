## Block on a rough incline

<Scenario id="incline-friction">
<div slot="intuition">
Put a block on a gentle slope and friction holds it still. Tilt the slope more and at some angle it lets go.
That angle depends only on the roughness, not on the mass: a heavy block and a light one slip at the same tilt.
Once it slides, kinetic friction is a little weaker than the static friction that was holding it, so it does not just
creep, it accelerates. Drag the angle slider past the critical value and watch the free-body diagram switch.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
Along the slope gravity contributes $m g \sin\theta$; the normal force is $N = m g \cos\theta$. At rest, static friction
equals $m g \sin\theta$ and cannot exceed $\mu_s N$, so the block stays until

$$
\tan\theta = \mu_s .
$$

Sliding, kinetic friction is $\mu_k N$ and the acceleration is $a = g(\sin\theta - \mu_k \cos\theta)$.
</div>
<div slot="engine">
Friction is decided per contact point every step the way a student is taught: compute the force that would hold the
block, compare it with $\mu_s N$, and if it is too much, switch to $\mu_k N$ opposing the motion. The engine reports
the actual friction force, so at rest the free-body diagram shows $m g \sin\theta$, not $\mu_s N$.
Validated to 1e-10 in both regimes.
</div>
</Scenario>

## Pushing a block on a rough floor

<Scenario id="pushed-block">
<div slot="intuition">
Push gently and nothing happens, yet something is happening: friction is pushing back with exactly your force.
Push harder and friction grows to match, until it cannot. Then the block moves, and because kinetic friction is weaker
than the maximum static friction, it accelerates the moment it starts.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
The block moves when $F > \mu_s m g$. Once moving,

$$
a = \frac{F - \mu_k m g}{m} .
$$

</div>
<div slot="engine">
Below the threshold the engine's friction readout equals the push to 1e-10, which is the free-body diagram textbooks
draw. The slip is reported as a `friction_slip` event.
</div>
</Scenario>

## Atwood machine

<Scenario id="atwood">
<div slot="intuition">
Two masses hang on a string over a pulley. If they are equal, nothing moves. Make one heavier and it descends while
the other rises, both with the same acceleration, because the string keeps them moving together. The string's
tension is the same on both sides and lies between the two weights: more than the lighter weight (it is being pulled
up) and less than the heavier (it is being let down).
</div>
<div slot="maths">
Newton's law on each mass with a common acceleration $a$ and tension $T$:

$$
a = \frac{m_2 - m_1}{m_1 + m_2}\, g, \qquad T = \frac{2 m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\, g .
$$

</div>
<div slot="engine">
The string is a rope constraint: an inequality on the path length over the pulley's tangent geometry. The engine
solves for the tension that keeps the path length fixed, and reports it. Validated to round-off against both formulas.
Set the masses equal and the acceleration readout is exactly zero.
</div>
</Scenario>

## Block on a table and a hanging mass

<Scenario id="table-and-hanging-mass">
<div slot="intuition">
A block on a table is tied to a mass hanging over the edge. The hanging weight pulls the whole system, but it has to
accelerate the block as well as itself, so it falls more slowly than free fall. Add table friction and there is a point
where the hanging weight is not enough to get the block moving at all.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
With table friction $\mu$, the system moves if $m_2 g > \mu m_1 g$, with

$$
a = \frac{m_2 - \mu m_1}{m_1 + m_2}\, g, \qquad T = m_1 (a + \mu g) .
$$

The normal force on the block is $m_1 g$ regardless.
</div>
<div slot="engine">
The table is a static polygon with friction, the rope bends over a pulley at its edge. After the hanging mass lands
on the floor the rope goes slack, which the event log reports, and a frictionless block simply keeps sliding.
</div>
</Scenario>
