## Charging a capacitor

<Scenario id="charging-capacitor" circuits height={440}>
<div slot="intuition">
At the first instant the capacitor is empty and offers no opposition, so the whole battery voltage is across the
resistor and the current is as large as it will ever be. As charge collects on the plates the capacitor builds
up a voltage of its own, against the battery. Less is left over for the resistor, the current falls, and the
charging slows down. It never quite finishes. After a time $RC$ it is 63 % full, after five of them more than
99 %.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
$$
q = CV\left(1 - e^{-t/RC}\right), \qquad i = \frac VR\,e^{-t/RC}, \qquad \tau = RC .
$$

With 10 kΩ and 100 μF, $\tau = 1$ s. The energy stored at the end is $\tfrac12CV^2$; an equal amount has been
turned to heat in the resistor, whatever its resistance.
</div>
<div slot="engine">
The capacitor's law $i = C\,dv/dt$ is applied over each small step with both sides averaged over the step. The
voltage follows the exponential to within a few parts in a million of the battery's, and the time to reach
63.2 % is $RC$ to three figures or better.
</div>
</Scenario>

## Discharging a capacitor

<Scenario id="discharging-capacitor" circuits height={440}>
<div slot="intuition">
A charged capacitor is a small, short-lived battery. Connected across a resistor it drives a current, which
drains it, which lowers its voltage, which lowers the current. So it empties quickly at first and then more and
more slowly. In each interval $RC$ the voltage falls to the same fraction, 36.8 %, of what it was.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
$$
v = V_0\,e^{-t/RC}, \qquad t_{1/2} = RC\ln 2 .
$$
</div>
<div slot="engine">
Both the time to fall to 36.8 % and the time to fall to half match their formulas to three figures or better.
</div>
</Scenario>

## Capacitors in series

<Scenario id="capacitors-in-series" circuits height={440}>
<div slot="intuition">
Look at the wire joining the two capacitors. It is connected to nothing else, so it cannot gain or lose charge.
Whatever charge is pushed onto the first plate of C₁ drives an equal charge off its second plate, and that can
only go to the facing plate of C₂. So both capacitors end with exactly the same charge. The smaller one needs a
bigger voltage to hold it. Together they store less than either alone.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
$$
\frac1C = \frac1{C_1} + \frac1{C_2}, \qquad Q_1 = Q_2 = CV, \qquad V_k = \frac{Q}{C_k} .
$$
</div>
<div slot="engine">
A circuit of steady currents says nothing here, since no steady current flows. The engine charges the pair
through a 1 kΩ resistor from empty and reads them once they have settled; equal charges then follow from the
junction rule at every step, not from a formula.
</div>
</Scenario>

## Capacitors in parallel

<Scenario id="capacitors-in-parallel" circuits height={440}>
<div slot="intuition">
Each capacitor is connected straight across the battery and charges to the full voltage, as if the other were
not there. The larger one takes more charge. Together they act as one capacitor with plates as big as both.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
$$
C = C_1 + C_2, \qquad Q_k = C_kV .
$$
</div>
<div slot="engine">
Again charged through a resistor and read at the end. Two capacitors wired directly side by side state the same
fact twice at the starting instant, which the engine resolves by giving each plate a billionth of an ohm.
</div>
</Scenario>

## Current growing in an inductor

<Scenario id="lr-circuit" circuits height={440}>
<div slot="intuition">
A coil resists any change in the current through it, by setting up an EMF against the change. Switch on, and for
an instant that back EMF equals the battery and no current flows at all. Then the current starts to grow. As it
grows more slowly the coil objects less, and in the end the current is steady at $V/R$ and the coil is just a
piece of wire. It is the mirror image of a capacitor charging: there the current starts large and dies; here it
starts at nothing and builds.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
$$
i = \frac VR\left(1 - e^{-tR/L}\right), \qquad \tau = \frac LR, \qquad U = \tfrac12LI^2 .
$$
</div>
<div slot="engine">
The coil's law $v = L\,di/dt$ is averaged over each step as the capacitor's is. The largest voltage across the
coil is the battery's, at the first instant, and the time to 63.2 % of the final current is $L/R$ to three
figures or better.
</div>
</Scenario>
