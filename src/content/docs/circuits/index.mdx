These pages cover the circuits of NCERT class 12: current electricity, the capacitor half of the chapter on
potential, the inductor, and alternating current.

The engine knows two things about a circuit and nothing more. What flows into a junction flows out of it. And
each component ties the voltage across it to the current through it: $V = IR$ for a resistor, $I = C\,dV/dt$ for
a capacitor, $V = L\,dI/dt$ for an inductor. It writes those down for whatever circuit it is given and solves
them. The rules for series and parallel, the balance of a bridge, the time constant $RC$ and the impedance of an
LCR circuit are not in the program. They are what the solution shows, and each is checked against its formula.

## Reading a circuit

The symbols are the textbook's: a zigzag for a resistor, a long thin plate and a short thick one for a cell (the
long one is positive), two equal plates for a capacitor, a coil for an inductor, a circle with a wave for an
alternating source, and circles lettered A, V and G for the ammeter, voltmeter and galvanometer.

Press play and red dots drift round the wires. They move the way conventional current flows, from the positive
terminal round to the negative, and faster where there is more of it. The side panel lists the voltage across and
the current through every component at that moment.

## Signs

Every component has two ends. Its voltage is the first end above the second, and its current is counted from the
first to the second through it. A resistor's two numbers therefore always have the same sign. A cell that is
supplying current shows a **negative** current, because inside it the current runs from the negative plate to
the positive. A cell being charged by a stronger one shows a positive current.

## What is and is not here

- **Meters are ideal.** The ammeter has no resistance and the voltmeter takes no current. The galvanometer has
  50 Ω.
- **Wires have no resistance,** except the wire of the metre bridge and the potentiometer, which is the point of
  them.
- **Alternating circuits are shown in their steady state,** found with complex numbers. The moment of switching
  on is not shown for those.
- **Not built yet:** the transformer, diodes and rectifiers, the temperature dependence of resistance, drift
  velocity at the level of electrons, and a bench where you build a circuit of your own.
