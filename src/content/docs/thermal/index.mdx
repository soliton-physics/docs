These pages follow the NCERT class 11 chapters on thermodynamics and kinetic theory, and take the first steps
into statistical mechanics beyond them. Everything on them comes from one thing: a few hundred molecules in a
box, flying in straight lines and bouncing off the walls and off each other.

Nothing thermodynamic is put in by hand.

- **Temperature** is the mean kinetic energy of the molecules, $\tfrac32 kT$ each.
- **Pressure** is counted from their blows on the walls.
- **Work** is the energy a moving piston gives the molecules that hit it, one collision at a time.
- **Heat** is what a wall held at a temperature hands to a molecule, or takes from it, when the molecule leaves
  with a fresh speed for that temperature.

The gas laws, Maxwell's distribution, the adiabatic law and the Carnot efficiency are then things you watch come
out, not things the program was told.

## The size of things

The box is about 10 nanometres across and the clock runs in picoseconds: this is a real gas at its real size,
slowed down some fifty thousand million times. The molecules are argon atoms unless a page says otherwise,
moving at about 400 m/s at room temperature. With 300 of them in so small a box the pressure is several
atmospheres.

## A few hundred is not 10²³

A real gas has so many molecules that its pressure and temperature are perfectly steady. Three hundred molecules
are not like that, and it shows, honestly:

- **Everything flickers.** The pressure wobbles by a few per cent from moment to moment, and so does the
  temperature of a gas that is touching a heat reservoir. Measured values settle as they are averaged over time,
  and each answer is marked as agreeing when it is within a stated few per cent.
- **Heat is the noisiest number.** The energy the gas itself holds flickers by about 5 %, and whatever it happens
  to hold at the start and end of a stroke shows up in the heat for that stroke. Work is much steadier.
- **Molecules have a size.** They are small hard spheres, so at high density the pressure is a per cent or two
  above $NkT/V$. That is the start of the van der Waals correction, and it is real.

## What is and is not here

- **The gas is monatomic.** Hard spheres cannot spin or vibrate, so $\gamma = 5/3$ and $C_V = \tfrac32 Nk$.
  Diatomic gases, with $\gamma = 7/5$, are not modelled.
- **No attraction between molecules,** so no liquid, no condensation and no critical point yet.
- **Pistons move slowly but not infinitely slowly:** about a hundredth of the speed of the molecules. A faster
  piston does visibly more work than the reversible formulas say, which is itself worth showing a class.
- **Not built yet:** heat conduction along a temperature gradient, specific heats of solids, the Ising model and
  Monte Carlo methods, phase transitions, and fluids. They are on the [roadmap](/roadmap/).
