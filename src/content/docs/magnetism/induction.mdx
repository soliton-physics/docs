A current makes a magnetic field. Faraday found the other half: a magnetic field makes a current, but only while
it is changing. What counts is the flux through the circuit, the field times the area it passes through.

$$
\mathcal{E} = -N\,\frac{d\Phi}{dt} , \qquad \Phi = BA\cos\theta .
$$

The minus sign is Lenz's law: the current that flows always opposes the change that caused it.

## A magnet through a coil

<Scenario id="faradays-law" magnetism>
<div slot="intuition">
Press play. As the magnet comes up to the coil the flux through the coil grows, and the gauge swings one way. At
the middle the flux is as large as it gets, but for a moment it is not changing, and the emf is zero. As the
magnet leaves, the flux falls and the gauge swings the other way.

Double the speed. The emf doubles, because the flux changes twice as fast, but it lasts half as long. The charge
that flows round is the same either way: it depends only on how much the flux changed.

The coil always resists. It pushes the magnet back as it arrives and pulls it back as it leaves.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
A small magnet of moment $m$ on the axis, a distance $z$ from a coil of radius $R$, sends through each turn

$$
\Phi = \frac{\mu_0 m R^2}{2\,(R^2+z^2)^{3/2}} .
$$

Moving at speed $v$, the emf is greatest at $z = R/2$, where

$$
\mathcal{E}_{\max} = \frac{24}{25\sqrt5}\,\frac{\mu_0 N m v}{R^2} .
$$

The charge that flows while the flux rises from nothing to its greatest is $Q = N\Phi_{\max}/R_{\text{circuit}}$,
with no speed in it.
</div>
<div slot="engine">
The engine moves the magnet in 4000 small steps, works out the flux before and after each, and divides the
change by the time. The largest emf found that way is compared with the formula, and the current is added up to
give the charge.
</div>
</Scenario>

## A rod on rails

<Scenario id="motional-emf" magnetism>
<div slot="intuition">
A rod is pulled along two rails by a steady force. The rails are joined through a resistance, and a magnetic
field passes through the circuit. As the rod moves the circuit's area grows, so its flux grows, so a current
flows.

That current, in the field, is pushed backwards. The faster the rod goes, the bigger the current and the harder
it is held back. Soon the braking equals the pull and the rod goes no faster.

Watch the second graph. At first most of the pull's work speeds the rod up. At the end all of it becomes heat in
the resistance. Nothing is lost: the answer chip keeps count.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
$$
\mathcal{E} = Blv , \qquad I = \frac{Blv}{R} , \qquad F_{\text{brake}} = BIl = \frac{B^2l^2v}{R} ,
$$

$$
v_{\text{terminal}} = \frac{FR}{B^2l^2} , \qquad \tau = \frac{mR}{B^2l^2} .
$$

At the terminal speed the power of the pull, $Fv$, equals the heating, $I^2R$.
</div>
<div slot="engine">
The rod is stepped forward by fourth-order Runge–Kutta for seven time constants. The distance and the heat are
added up along the way, and the work done by the pull is compared with the heat plus the rod's kinetic energy.
</div>
</Scenario>

## A loop falling through a field

<Scenario id="falling-loop" magnetism>
<div slot="intuition">
A square loop of wire is dropped through a band of magnetic field. There are three stages.

**Going in.** Only the bottom edge is in the field. The flux through the loop is growing, a current flows, and
the loop is braked. The graph of speed bends away from the dashed line of free fall.

**Wholly inside.** The flux is large but steady. No current flows and the loop falls freely again. This is the
part people get wrong: a strong field does nothing by being strong.

**Coming out.** The flux falls, the current flows the other way, and the loop is braked again.

Set the field to zero and it falls freely all the way. This braking with no touching is how the brakes on some
trains and roller coasters work.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
While one edge of length $l$ cuts the field,

$$
I = \frac{Blv}{R} , \qquad m\frac{dv}{dt} = mg - \frac{B^2l^2}{R}\,v ,
\qquad v \to \frac{mgR}{B^2l^2} .
$$

Energy is conserved throughout: the heat $\int I^2R\,dt$ equals the potential energy lost less the kinetic
energy gained.
</div>
<div slot="engine">
The loop falls freely unless exactly one of its horizontal edges is inside the band. While braked, its speed
closes on the terminal speed exponentially, and the engine takes that step exactly, so even a very strong field
causes no numerical trouble. The heat is compared with the energy the fall has lost.
</div>
</Scenario>

## The AC generator

<Scenario id="ac-generator" magnetism>
<div slot="intuition">
The coil is seen along its axle, as a line, turning between the poles. The dashed arrow is its normal.

When the coil faces the field, the flux through it is greatest, but its sides are moving along the field and cut
nothing: the emf is zero. A quarter turn later the coil lies along the field, the flux is zero, the sides cut
straight across it, and the emf is at its peak. Half a turn on, the sides have changed places and the emf is
reversed.

Turn it faster and the peak rises in proportion.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
$$
\Phi = NBA\cos\omega t \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal{E} = NBA\omega\,\sin\omega t , \qquad \mathcal{E}_0 = NBA\omega , \qquad
\mathcal{E}_{\text{rms}} = \frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{\sqrt2} .
$$
</div>
<div slot="engine">
The flux is evaluated 2000 times in each turn and the emf taken from its change. The largest value in a full
turn is compared with $NBA\omega$.
</div>
</Scenario>

## Self-inductance

<Scenario id="self-inductance" magnetism>
<div slot="intuition">
A coil's own field passes through its own turns. Change the current and that flux changes, so the coil induces
an emf in itself, always against the change.

Press play. While the current rises the emf opposes it. While the current is steady there is no emf at all.
While the current falls the emf turns round and tries to keep it going. That reluctance to change is inductance,
and it is to a current what mass is to a velocity.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
For a solenoid of $N$ turns, length $l$ and cross-section $A$:

$$
L = \frac{\mu_0 N^2 A}{l} , \qquad \mathcal{E} = -L\,\frac{dI}{dt} , \qquad U = \tfrac12 L I^2 .
$$
</div>
<div slot="engine">
The emf is taken from the change in the flux through all the turns. While the current rises, the work done
pushing it against that emf is added up and compared with $\tfrac12LI^2$.
</div>
</Scenario>

## Mutual inductance

<Scenario id="mutual-inductance" magnetism>
<div slot="intuition">
A second coil is wound round the first. No wire joins them, but the first coil's field passes through the
second. An alternating current in the first makes an alternating emf in the second.

Compare the two gauges. The emf is greatest when the current is passing through zero, because that is when it is
changing fastest, and zero when the current is at its peak.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
For a short coil of $N_2$ turns round a long solenoid of $N_1$ turns, length $l$ and cross-section $A$:

$$
M = \frac{\mu_0 N_1 N_2 A}{l} , \qquad \mathcal{E}_2 = -M\,\frac{dI_1}{dt} ,
\qquad I_1 = I_0\sin\omega t \ \Longrightarrow\ \mathcal{E}_2 = -M\omega I_0\cos\omega t .
$$
</div>
<div slot="engine">
The flux of the first coil through the second is evaluated 2000 times a cycle. The largest emf is compared with
$M\omega I_0$.
</div>
</Scenario>

## The transformer

<Scenario id="transformer" magnetism>
<div slot="intuition">
Put both coils on one iron core and nearly all the flux of one passes through the other. Every turn of either
coil then has the same emf in it, so the voltages are in the ratio of the turns.

A transformer does not make power. Ten times the voltage comes with a tenth of the current. That is the point:
the heat wasted in a power line depends on the current squared, so sending the same power at ten times the
voltage wastes a hundredth as much. Lower the secondary turns until the line loses most of what is sent.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
$$
\frac{V_s}{V_p} = \frac{N_s}{N_p} , \qquad V_pI_p = V_sI_s , \qquad
P_{\text{lost}} = I^2R_{\text{line}} = \left(\frac{P}{V}\right)^2 R_{\text{line}} .
$$
</div>
<div slot="engine">
These are the ideal transformer's laws evaluated. Sending 800 kW at 4000 V down a 15 Ω line would waste 600 kW;
stepped up to 40 kV it wastes 6 kW.
</div>
</Scenario>
