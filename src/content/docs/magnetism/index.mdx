These pages cover the second half of the NCERT class 12 chapter on moving charges and magnetism, the chapter on
electromagnetic induction, and the transformer. They are in the Magnetism part of the playground.

[Electromagnetism](/electromagnetism/) dealt with charges moving in a field that was simply given. These pages
are about where a magnetic field comes from, which is a current, and about what a changing field does, which is
to drive a current. Between them those two facts are the motor, the generator and the transformer.

## What is computed and what is a formula

The textbook gives a formula for each case. The engine gets the same number another way, and shows both.

- **Fields are summed from the Biot–Savart law.** The wire is cut into thousands of short pieces and the field
  of each is added up. The compass needles in the pictures come from the same sum, so they are right off the
  axis too, where the textbook has no formula.
- **Every emf is taken from the change of flux.** The engine works out the flux through the circuit at one
  moment and at the next, and divides the change by the time. That is Faraday's law used directly.
- **Energy is counted.** For the rod on rails and the falling loop, the heat made in the resistance is added up
  and compared with the work done or the energy lost.

The force on a wire and the transformer are laws evaluated, with a picture.

## Limits

- **Wires are long and thin, and coils are flat.** The solenoid's ends are counted in its field, but not in its
  inductance.
- **The magnet in Faraday's law is a point dipole.** A real bar magnet gives a wider, flatter pulse.
- **The transformer is ideal.** It has no losses of its own; only the power line wastes anything.
- **Not built yet:** the toroid, a motor with a commutator, and eddy currents in a solid plate.
