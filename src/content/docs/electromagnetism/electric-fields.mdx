## Force between two charges

<Scenario id="coulomb-force">
<div slot="intuition">
Two like charges, one fixed and one free. The free one is pushed away, hardest at the start when they are closest,
and less and less as it goes. Halve the starting distance and the push is four times as large. Because the force
never quite vanishes the charge keeps gaining speed for ever, but less and less: there is a speed it approaches
and never passes.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
$$
F = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}, \qquad \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} = 8.99 \times 10^9\ \mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}.
$$

The potential energy of the pair is $U = q_1 q_2 / 4\pi\varepsilon_0 r$. Released from rest at $r_0$, energy gives the
speed at any distance:

$$
\tfrac{1}{2} m v^2 = \frac{q_1 q_2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\left(\frac{1}{r_0} - \frac{1}{r}\right).
$$

At $r = 2r_0$ it already has half of all the kinetic energy it will ever have.
</div>
<div slot="engine">
Coulomb's law is a force between every pair of charged bodies, with its potential energy in the ledger. A charged
body that is fixed in place is felt but not moved. The force at the first instant matches the formula to the last
digit, the speed at twice the distance to two parts in a thousand (the instant is caught between two steps), and
the total energy holds to one part in ten million.
</div>
</Scenario>

## Field of two charges

<Scenario id="field-of-two-charges">
<div slot="intuition">
The field at a point is the force a small positive test charge would feel there, per unit of its charge. The lines
show its direction everywhere: out of positive charge, into negative. Make the two charges alike and the lines
push apart, leaving a point between them where the field is zero. The test charge starts off along the line
through it, but it does not follow the line: a field line shows force, not path.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
Fields add as vectors, potentials as numbers:

$$
\mathbf{E} = \sum_i \frac{q_i}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_i}{|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_i|^3},
\qquad V = \sum_i \frac{q_i}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 |\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_i|}.
$$

For equal and opposite charges the potential is zero everywhere on the line midway between them, while the field
there is not: it points straight across, from the positive charge to the negative.
</div>
<div slot="engine">
The engine can be asked for the field and the potential at any point; the playground does so wherever the pointer
is. Field lines are traced along the field in small steps, starting on positive charges in number proportional to
the charge. They are a drawing: their direction is exact, their number is a choice, and their spacing is only a
rough guide to strength in a flat picture of a field that really spreads in three dimensions.
</div>
</Scenario>

## Charged bob in an electric field

<Scenario id="charged-pendulum">
<div slot="intuition">
A charged bob on a thread, in a field that pulls it sideways. There is an angle at which it could hang at rest,
where the thread lines up against weight and electric force together. Let go from straight below and it does not
stop there: it swings through and reaches exactly twice that angle, then comes back. It is a pendulum whose
"down" has been tilted.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
At rest, $T\sin\theta = qE$ and $T\cos\theta = mg$:

$$
\tan\theta = \frac{qE}{mg}, \qquad T = \sqrt{(mg)^2 + (qE)^2}.
$$

Weight and electric force together act like a single gravity of strength $g' = \sqrt{g^2 + (qE/m)^2}$ pointing
along that angle, so small swings about it have the period $2\pi\sqrt{L/g'}$.
</div>
<div slot="engine">
A uniform field that fills the plane has a potential energy, $-q\,\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{r}$, so the ledger treats
it exactly as it treats gravity. The furthest angle matches $2\theta$ to one part in ten thousand. Placed at
$\theta$ and released, the bob does not move at all and the thread reads the tension above to nine figures.
</div>
</Scenario>

## A charge fired between plates

<Scenario id="charge-between-plates">
<div slot="intuition">
Between the plates the charge is pushed sideways at a steady rate while it carries on forward at a steady speed.
That is a ball thrown level off a cliff, with the field in place of gravity, and the path is the same parabola.
Past the plates nothing pushes it and it goes straight, to a spot on the screen. This is how an oscilloscope
steers its beam. Make the charge negative and it bends the other way.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
With $a = qE/m$ and a time $L/v$ between plates of length $L$:

$$
y = \frac{qEL^2}{2mv^2}, \qquad \tan\phi = \frac{qEL}{mv^2}.
$$

On a screen a distance $D$ beyond the plates the spot is at $y + D\tan\phi = \dfrac{qEL}{mv^2}\left(\dfrac{L}{2} + D\right)$.
</div>
<div slot="engine">
The field exists only inside a rectangle. The charge crosses its edge part-way through a time step, so this
scenario uses a step four times smaller than usual, and the deflection is right to three parts in a thousand. A
field that stops dead at the edge of the plates has no potential energy, so the work it does is booked as coming
from outside, from whatever keeps the plates charged.
</div>
</Scenario>

## Balancing a charged drop

<Scenario id="balanced-drop">
<div slot="intuition">
A charged drop in an upward field. Too little field and it falls, too much and it rises. Find the field at which
it hangs still: there the electric force is exactly its weight, and if you know the field and the weight you know
the charge. This is the idea of Millikan's experiment, which found that charge comes in whole numbers of one small
amount.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
Balance: $qE = mg$, so $E = mg/q$. Otherwise the drop accelerates upward at

$$
a = \frac{qE}{m} - g.
$$
</div>
<div slot="engine">
At the balancing field the drop's speed stays below a billionth of a metre per second for as long as you watch.
Away from it the acceleration matches the formula to nine figures and the energy ledger closes exactly: both
forces are uniform, and velocity Verlet is exact for constant acceleration.
</div>
</Scenario>

## A dipole in a uniform field

<Scenario id="dipole-in-field">
<div slot="intuition">
Equal and opposite charges on the ends of a light rod. The field pushes one end forward and the other back with
equal forces, so the rod as a whole goes nowhere. But the two pushes do not line up, and together they twist it
round towards the field. It swings through, overshoots and swings back, like a compass needle settling.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
With dipole moment $p = qd$ at angle $\theta$ to the field:

$$
\tau = pE\sin\theta, \qquad U = -pE\cos\theta.
$$

For small angles the twist is proportional to the angle, so the motion is simple harmonic with

$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I}{pE}}, \qquad I = 2m\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)^2.
$$
</div>
<div slot="engine">
The dipole is two charged beads joined by a rod constraint; no torque is put in by hand. The middle of the rod
stays where it is to a billionth of a metre, the period at a small angle matches the formula to two parts in a
thousand, and the energy holds to a part in a million. Released at 30° the period is about 2 % longer than the
small-angle formula, as a pendulum's would be.
</div>
</Scenario>

## Gauss's law

<Scenario id="gauss-law">
<div slot="intuition">
Draw any closed surface, a bag with no holes, and add up the electric field coming out through every patch of it.
Gauss's law says the total depends on one thing only: how much charge is inside the bag. Drag the dashed surface
over the positive charge and the flux jumps to that charge over ε₀. Stretch it round both charges and it reads
their sum. Move it off to one side with nothing inside and it reads zero, though field lines are streaming through
it: every line that goes in comes out again.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
The flux through a small flat patch is $E\,\Delta A\cos\theta$. Over a whole closed surface

$$
\Phi = \oint \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{A} = \frac{q_\text{inside}}{\varepsilon_0}.
$$

The surface here is a sphere, of which the page shows the circle. Its size and position do not matter, only what
it holds.
</div>
<div slot="engine">
The measured flux is not worked out from the charge inside, which would only be the law quoted back. The engine
cuts the sphere into forty thousand patches, works out the field at each from Coulomb's law, and adds up
$\mathbf{E}\cdot\Delta\mathbf{A}$. That sum agrees with $q/\varepsilon_0$ to better than a part in a thousand,
and falls to a thousandth of it for a surface with no charge inside. With a charge sitting almost on the surface
the patches are too coarse and the sum is less exact.
</div>
</Scenario>

## Circling a charged wire

<Scenario id="charged-wire">
<div slot="intuition">
A long straight wire with charge all along it, seen end on, so it is the ringed plus in the middle. Its field lines
spread out in a flat fan and not in all directions, so the field weakens as $1/r$ and not $1/r^2$. That gives an
odd result. Move the bead further out: it circles at exactly the same speed as before, and only takes longer
because the circle is bigger.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
Take a cylinder of radius $r$ and length $L$ round the wire. The field crosses only its curved side, so
$E \cdot 2\pi r L = \lambda L/\varepsilon_0$:

$$
E = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 r}.
$$

For an opposite charge $q$ in a circle, $mv^2/r = qE$ and the $r$ cancels:

$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{q\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 m}}.
$$
</div>
<div slot="engine">
The wire is a fixed line of charge through the page. At radii of 0.5 m and 1.2 m the bead holds its circle and its
speed to a part in ten thousand, and its energy to a part in a million. The potential of a wire has no zero at
infinity; the engine puts it one metre from the wire.
</div>
</Scenario>

## Beside a charged sheet

<Scenario id="charged-sheet">
<div slot="intuition">
A large flat sheet of charge, seen edge on. Its field lines cannot spread at all: they leave straight out from both
faces. So the field is the same one metre away as one centimetre away. Start the bead anywhere and it speeds up at
the same steady rate. Now make the bead's charge negative. It is pulled to the sheet, passes through a small hole
in it, is pulled back from the other side, and goes to and fro.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
A pillbox through the sheet, with faces of area $A$, holds charge $\sigma A$ and has flux through its two faces:

$$
2EA = \frac{\sigma A}{\varepsilon_0} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad E = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}.
$$

The force on the bead, $q\sigma/2\varepsilon_0$, is constant, so the motion is that of a ball under gravity. An
opposite charge let go at distance $d$ comes back after $4\sqrt{2d/a}$: steady acceleration all the way, so it is
not simple harmonic.
</div>
<div slot="engine">
The sheet goes on for ever in the model. A bead let go 0.3 m away and one let go 1.5 m away cover
$\tfrac12 at^2$ with the same $a$ to a part in a million. For the opposite charge the force flips the instant it
crosses, which no time step lands on exactly: after a whole trip it is back within a centimetre of where it
started, and the energy holds to a part in a hundred over two trips.
</div>
</Scenario>

## Inside a charged shell

<Scenario id="charged-shell">
<div slot="intuition">
A hollow sphere with charge spread evenly over it. Inside there is no field anywhere, not just at the centre: the
near part of the shell pushes harder, but there is more of the far part, and the two cancel exactly. Watch the bead
cross the inside in a dead straight line at a steady speed, with the force reading zero. The moment it is out
through the hole, it is pushed away as though all the shell's charge sat at the centre.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
A spherical surface of radius $r$, concentric with the shell of radius $R$ and charge $Q$:

$$
E = 0 \quad (r < R), \qquad E = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r^2} \quad (r > R).
$$

No field inside means the potential there is the same everywhere, $Q/4\pi\varepsilon_0 R$. So the bead leaves with

$$
\tfrac12 mv^2 = \tfrac12 mv_0^2 + \frac{qQ}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\left(\frac1R - \frac1r\right).
$$
</div>
<div slot="engine">
Inside, the bead's velocity stays what it was to the last digit. It reaches the shell at the time a straight path
at steady speed gives, and its speed afterwards matches the energy equation to a part in a thousand. Field lines
are drawn for the shell alone, starting on its surface, without the bead's own.
</div>
</Scenario>
