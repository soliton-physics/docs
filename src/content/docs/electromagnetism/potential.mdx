## Closest approach to a fixed charge

<Scenario id="closest-approach">
<div slot="intuition">
A charge fired straight at a like charge that is held in place. It slows all the way in, because it is climbing a
hill of potential energy, stops for an instant, and is thrown back out with the speed it came in at. How close it
gets depends only on how much kinetic energy it had. Rutherford used exactly this to put a ceiling on the size of
the nucleus: an alpha particle of known energy turned back at a distance he could work out.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
Energy at the start equals energy at the turning point, where the speed is zero:

$$
\tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 + \frac{qQ}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r_0} = \frac{qQ}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r_\text{min}}.
$$

Fired from far away, $r_\text{min} = qQ / (4\pi\varepsilon_0 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2)$.
</div>
<div slot="engine">
Nothing tells the engine where to stop the charge; it steps Coulomb's law forward and the turning point comes out.
The closest distance matches the energy argument to one part in a hundred thousand. Open the Energy tab while it
runs: kinetic drains into potential and comes back, and the total does not move.
</div>
</Scenario>

## Speeding up through a potential difference

<Scenario id="accelerating-voltage">
<div slot="intuition">
A charge starts at rest at one plate and is pushed across to the other. Move the plates further apart and the
field between them is weaker, but it acts over a longer distance, and the two effects cancel exactly: the charge
leaves at the same speed. Only the potential difference matters. That is why the energy of a particle from an
accelerator is quoted in volts.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
The work done on the charge is $qV$, all of which becomes kinetic energy:

$$
qV = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2, \qquad v = \sqrt{\frac{2qV}{m}}.
$$

The field is $E = V/d$, and the crossing takes $t = d\sqrt{2m/qV}$: the gap changes the time, not the speed.
</div>
<div slot="engine">
The field exists only between the plates, so its work is booked as external, done by the supply. With gaps of
0.5 m and 2 m the leaving speed is the same to a part in a thousand, and the ledger's external work equals the
kinetic energy gained to a part in a million.
</div>
</Scenario>
