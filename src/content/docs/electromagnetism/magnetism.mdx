On this page the magnetic field points out of the page, towards you, shown as dots. A positive charge moving in
the page is then pushed to its own right, and turns clockwise.

## A charge in a magnetic field

<Scenario id="charge-in-magnetic-field">
<div slot="intuition">
A magnetic field pushes a moving charge sideways, always at right angles to the way it is going. A push like that
can turn it but can never speed it up or slow it down, so the charge goes round in a circle at constant speed.
Now double the speed: the circle is twice as big, and the charge takes exactly as long to go round it. Watch the
"time for one turn" stay put while you move the speed slider.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
The magnetic force $qvB$ supplies the centripetal force $mv^2/r$:

$$
r = \frac{mv}{qB}, \qquad T = \frac{2\pi r}{v} = \frac{2\pi m}{qB}.
$$

The speed has cancelled out of the period. The force does no work, since $\mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{v} = 0$ always.
</div>
<div slot="engine">
The magnetic force depends on velocity, so the second half of each step is solved for the new velocity instead of
using the old one. For a pure turn that rule rotates the velocity without changing its length: the speed is
constant to the last digit, not just approximately. The radius is right to one part in a hundred thousand, and
the ledger records zero work from the field.
</div>
</Scenario>

## Velocity selector

<Scenario id="velocity-selector">
<div slot="intuition">
Between these plates there are two fields at once. The electric field pushes the charge up with the same force
whatever its speed. The magnetic field pushes it down, harder the faster it goes. At exactly one speed the two
cancel and the charge goes straight through to the middle of the screen. Faster charges are bent down, slower ones
up. Put a slit at the middle and only one speed gets through.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
Straight through when $qE = qvB$:

$$
v = \frac{E}{B}.
$$

Neither the charge nor the mass appears: the selector picks a speed, whatever the particle.
</div>
<div slot="engine">
Both fields are confined to the same rectangle. At $v = E/B$ the charge lands within a billionth of a metre of the
centre line. One metre per second faster and it lands well below it, one slower and well above, as it should.
</div>
</Scenario>

## Cyclotron

<Scenario id="cyclotron">
<div slot="intuition">
A magnet bends the charge round and brings it back to a narrow gap twice in every turn. Across the gap there is a
voltage that gives it a kick. The trick is in the last section: a turn takes the same time however fast the charge
is going, so a voltage that simply alternates at that one fixed rate is pointing the right way every time the
charge arrives. Each kick widens the orbit, and the charge spirals out until the magnet can no longer hold it.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
The voltage must alternate at the cyclotron frequency

$$
f = \frac{qB}{2\pi m}.
$$

Each crossing adds at most $qV$ of kinetic energy. The largest orbit the magnet holds has radius $R$, so

$$
v_\text{max} = \frac{qBR}{m}, \qquad K_\text{max} = \frac{q^2B^2R^2}{2m}.
$$
</div>
<div slot="engine">
The field in the gap alternates as a cosine and has no potential, so every joule the charge gains is booked as
work from the supply; the ledger's external work equals the kinetic energy to a part in ten thousand. Successive
turns take the same time to within 3 %, the gap's width being the reason it is not exact. The magnet here is a
square, and the charge leaves when its orbit outgrows it, a little before $qBR/m$: the playground shows that
figure as the most the magnet can hold, with the measured speed beside it.
</div>
</Scenario>
