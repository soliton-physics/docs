## What the playground gives you

- **Read any point.** Click anywhere on a wave and the panel shows that point's displacement, the velocity and
  acceleration of the string or the air there, the amplitude it swings with, and its phase. Under them is a small
  graph of that one point over the last few cycles. Drag the point along the wave to watch them change.
- **Compare two points.** Click a second point and you get the distance between them, as metres and as a fraction of a
  wavelength, and the phase difference. Half a wavelength apart reads "exactly out of step".
- **The numbers worth knowing.** Wavelength, frequency, period, speed, wave number and angular frequency are always on
  screen, worked out from the sliders, with the formulas typeset underneath. On the wave itself the wavelength and
  amplitude are marked, and nodes and antinodes are labelled N and A.
- **Slow motion, honestly labelled.** A 170 Hz sound wave is shown about five hundred times slower than life, and the
  clock says so.
- **Remix.** Every wave is a sum of sine waves and pulses. Press Remix and it opens in the wave lab as its parts: change
  each one, send it the other way, add more. A standing wave comes apart into the two travelling waves that make it.
  The link in the address bar carries your mixture, so a lab can be handed to a class.

## How these are computed

The waves on these pages are closed formulas, sums of $A\sin(kx \mp \omega t + \varphi)$ and of pulses
$f(x \mp vt)$, evaluated in your browser at each instant. Nothing is integrated step by step, so there is no numerical
error to speak of: a reading is the formula's value. That is also why any point can be read at any time.

Reflection is done the way the textbook argues it. The wall is replaced by a mirror-image pulse coming from behind
it, upside down for a fixed end and upright for a free one, and the two are added.

The [solitons](/physics/kdv/) are a different matter. They obey nonlinear equations with no such shortcut, and are
integrated by the engine.
