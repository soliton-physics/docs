## Two waves add

<Wave id="superposition">
<div slot="intuition">
Send two waves down the same string and the string does not choose between them. At every point it moves by what the
first asks plus what the second asks. That is the whole principle of superposition.

When the two are in step, crest on crest, they build a wave twice as tall. Half a cycle apart, crest on trough, they
cancel and the string lies still, although two waves are passing through it. In between you get something in between.
The faint lines are the two waves; the bright one is the string.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
For two equal waves a phase $\varphi$ apart:

$$
y = A\sin(kx - \omega t) + A\sin(kx - \omega t + \varphi)
  = 2A\cos\frac{\varphi}{2}\,\sin\!\Big(kx - \omega t + \frac{\varphi}{2}\Big) .
$$

For unequal ones the amplitude of the sum is

$$
A_R = \sqrt{A_1^2 + A_2^2 + 2A_1A_2\cos\varphi},
$$

largest, $A_1 + A_2$, at $\varphi = 0, 2\pi, \dots$ and smallest, $|A_1 - A_2|$, at $\varphi = \pi, 3\pi, \dots$
</div>
<div slot="try">
- Slide the phase difference to 180° with equal amplitudes: the bright line goes flat.
- Make the amplitudes unequal and try 180° again. They can no longer cancel completely; what is left is the difference.
- At 120° with equal amplitudes the sum has the same amplitude as each wave. Check it against $2A\cos(\varphi/2)$.
</div>
</Wave>

## Two pulses cross

<Wave id="pulses-crossing">
<div slot="intuition">
Two pulses run at each other along one string. While they overlap, their heights add. Then they separate and each
carries on with the shape and speed it had, as if it had met nothing.

Set one up and one down and at one instant the string is completely straight. It has not stopped: every piece of it
is moving. All the energy is in that motion for that moment, and it is what rebuilds the two pulses on the far side.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
Any shape moving right and any shape moving left can share a string:

$$
y(x,t) = f(x - vt) + g(x + vt) .
$$

With $g = -f$ mirrored, $y = 0$ everywhere at the instant of overlap, while $\partial y/\partial t \neq 0$.
</div>
<div slot="try">
- Pause when the string is flat, click a point near the middle and read its velocity. It is far from zero.
- Make both heights positive. At the overlap the string reaches their sum.
- Press Remix in the playground and add a third pulse.
</div>
</Wave>

## Reflection at a fixed end

<Wave id="reflection-fixed">
<div slot="intuition">
The string is tied to a wall. When the pulse arrives it pulls up on the wall. The wall does not move, so it pulls down
on the string just as hard, and that downward tug is what starts the pulse on its way back: upside down.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
The end cannot move, $y(L,t) = 0$ at all times. That is satisfied by adding to the incoming pulse its mirror image,
turned over:

$$
y(x,t) = f(x - vt) - f(2L - x - vt) .
$$

Turning a sine wave over is the same as shifting it by half a cycle: reflection at a fixed end changes the phase by
$\pi$.
</div>
<div slot="try">
- Switch on "show each wave" in the playground's dock. The mirror-image pulse comes out of the wall as the real one
  goes in.
- Click a point right at the wall and watch its reading through the whole reflection: zero.
</div>
</Wave>

## Reflection at a free end

<Wave id="reflection-free">
<div slot="intuition">
Now the string ends in a light ring that slides freely up a smooth rod. Nothing holds the end down. When the pulse
arrives the ring is flung up, and with nothing beyond it to pull against it overshoots to twice the height of the
pulse. As it comes down it pulls the string along behind it and sends the pulse back the same way up.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
A free end can have no sideways force on it, so the string must meet the rod level:
$\partial y/\partial x = 0$ at $x = L$. The mirror image is now added without turning it over:

$$
y(x,t) = f(x - vt) + f(2L - x - vt), \qquad y(L,t) = 2f(L - vt) .
$$

There is no change of phase.
</div>
<div slot="try">
- Watch the ring: it rises to twice the pulse's height. The panel gives the number.
- Compare with the fixed end, same pulse. Same speed, same shape coming back, one upside down and one not.
</div>
</Wave>
