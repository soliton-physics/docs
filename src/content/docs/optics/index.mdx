These pages follow the NCERT class 12 chapter on ray optics and optical instruments. Light here is a ray: a
straight line that turns only where it meets a mirror, a lens, or the surface between two materials. The engine
follows each ray to the point where it actually strikes and applies the law of reflection or Snell's law there,
exactly, with no small-angle shortcut.

On every bench you can drag the round knob: the top of the object, the start of a ray, the eyepiece. The picture
is traced again as you move it. Beside each textbook answer is the same quantity **traced**, read off the rays
themselves.

## Signs

Distances follow the textbook's convention. Light travels from left to right. Distances are measured from the pole
of a mirror or the centre of a lens: positive to the right, negative to the left, heights positive upward. So an
object on the left is at a negative $u$, a concave mirror has a negative focal length, and a real image in a
mirror has a negative $v$.

## Two kinds of image

The mirror and lens formulas are about rays close to the axis and objects of small height. The image the engine
reports, and draws as the blue arrow, is that one: it follows two rays a hair either side of the axis and finds
where they cross. A dashed arrow is a virtual image, with dashed lines showing the rays produced backwards to it.

The red rays you see are the real ones, traced exactly from the top of the object. For a spherical mirror they
miss the blue arrow's tip by a little when the object is tall, because a spherical mirror does not bring wide rays
to exactly one point. That is spherical aberration, and it is real.

## What is and is not here

- **Mirrors and glass are real surfaces.** Reflection and refraction are worked out at the true point of contact.
- **The lenses in most scenes are ideal thin lenses,** a line that turns every ray by exactly what the lens formula
  asks. They have no thickness and no aberration. The lens maker's scene is the exception: it is a real lens of
  glass, traced face by face.
- **Each ray either reflects or refracts.** The faint reflection off a glass surface that also transmits is not
  drawn, and neither is absorption. The rainbow is the one exception: there the reflected part inside the drop
  is the part that is followed.
- **One colour, except where colour is the subject.** In the dispersion and rainbow scenes the glass and the
  water have an index for each wavelength. Everywhere else light is of no particular colour, so lenses show no
  coloured fringes.
- **Not built yet:** the eye and its defects, the simple microscope on its own, reflecting telescopes, a plane
  mirror scene, and a bench where you place your own mirrors and lenses.
