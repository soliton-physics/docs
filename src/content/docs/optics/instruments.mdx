Both instruments are two lenses on one axis. The lenses here are ideal thin lenses, so what you see is how the
instrument works, without the aberrations a designer then has to fight.

## An astronomical telescope

<Scenario id="telescope" optics>
<div slot="intuition">
Light from a star arrives parallel. The objective, a lens of long focal length, brings it to a point in its focal
plane. The eyepiece, a lens of short focal length, is placed with its own focus at that same point, so it sends the
light on parallel again, into a relaxed eye. But the light leaves at a much steeper angle than it arrived. Two
stars that were a little apart in the sky are far apart in the eyepiece.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
In normal adjustment the lenses are $f_o + f_e$ apart and

$$
m = \frac{\beta}{\alpha} = \frac{f_o}{f_e} .
$$

A 40 cm objective with a 5 cm eyepiece magnifies 8 times in a tube 45 cm long. The image is upside down.
</div>
<div slot="engine">
The magnifying power is read off one ray: the slope it leaves the eyepiece with over the slope it arrived with.
It matches $f_o/f_e$ to round-off.
</div>
</Scenario>

## A compound microscope

<Scenario id="compound-microscope" optics height={460}>
<div slot="intuition">
The object sits just beyond the focus of the objective, a lens of very short focal length, which makes a real,
enlarged image of it some way up the tube. The eyepiece is a magnifying glass held up to that image, close enough
that the final image is virtual and stands at the distance of comfortable close viewing, 25 cm. The enlargement
of the objective is multiplied by that of the eyepiece.
</div>
<div slot="maths">
$$
m = m_o \times m_e = \frac{v_o}{u_o}\left(1 + \frac{D}{f_e}\right), \qquad D = 25\ \text{cm}.
$$

With $f_o = 2$ cm and the object 2.5 cm away, $v_o = 10$ cm and $m_o = -4$. With $f_e = 5$ cm, $m_e = 6$. Together,
24 times, inverted.
</div>
<div slot="engine">
The final image is found by following near-axis rays through both lenses and producing them back. Its height over
the object's matches the product of the two formulas to a part in a million. Move the object closer to the focus
and the first image, and the tube, get longer.
</div>
</Scenario>
